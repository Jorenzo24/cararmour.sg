What interior detailing covers

An interior detail goes further than a vacuum and a quick wipe. Every material in the cabin is cleaned with a method of its own, and the places a normal clean misses get attention by hand: air vents, seat rails, door pockets, the gaps around the gear lever and the seams of the seats, where dust and crumbs settle.

Using the wrong product is the most common way interiors get damaged. A household degreaser dries out leather, a wet brush leaves water marks on a headliner, and a glossy dressing on the dashboard reflects into the windscreen.

Humidity and the cabin

Singapore's mean relative humidity is around 82 percent, according to the Meteorological Service Singapore. The US Environmental Protection Agency advises keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent to limit mould. A closed car in a humid carpark sits well above that line.

Mould needs moisture and something organic to feed on: crumbs, spilled drinks, skin cells, the dust held in carpets. Damp floor mats and food residue in a closed cabin provide both, and a musty smell is usually the first sign. The air-conditioning system can add to it, because its evaporator stays wet while the car is in use.

Why steam is used

A steam cleaner produces vapour above 100 °C. Hot enough to sanitise surfaces, it lifts dirt from seams and textured plastics without soaking them in chemicals, and it dries quickly. That makes it well suited to cup holders, steering wheels, door handles and the other surfaces hands touch every day.

How an interior detail is done

The order matters: dry dirt comes out before anything gets wet, and the cabin is left to air before the doors are closed.

  1. Empty and dry vacuum

    Mats and loose items come out. Seats, carpets, rails and boot are vacuumed, with brushes to loosen what is stuck in the fibres.

  2. Steam and agitation

    Vents, seams, cup holders and controls are steamed and brushed to release dirt and sanitise them.

  3. Material by material

    Leather, fabric, plastics and glass are each cleaned with their own product, from top to bottom.

  4. Extraction

    Fabric seats, carpets and mats are shampooed and extracted with hot water, then left to dry.

  5. Final check and airing

    Glass is finished streak-free, and the doors stay open for a while so the cabin can dry out.

Keeping the cabin clean between details

A few habits make the result last much longer in Singapore's climate.

  • Dry the mats. Take wet floor mats out to dry rather than leaving them in a closed car.
  • No food left behind. Crumbs and drink spills are what feeds mould and odours.
  • Leather care. A wipe with a leather cleaner every few weeks and a conditioner every few months keep it supple.
  • A sunshade. It protects the dashboard and seats from UV and keeps the cabin cooler.
  • Air-conditioning. Running the fan with the compressor off for the last few minutes of a drive helps dry the evaporator.
