03 Coating

What a car coating actually does

A coating is not a wax. Wax sits on top of the clear coat and Singapore rain takes it off in weeks. A coating bonds chemically to the paint and stays for years. That is why it carries a written warranty and a wax does not.

Once it has cured, water beads and sheets straight off instead of drying into spots, the paint stops etching every time a bird or an afternoon storm gets to it, and washing takes half as long because nothing keys into the surface. We install two families.

What a coating will not do

It will not stop stone chips. A coating is measured in microns. It changes how the surface behaves chemically, not how it survives an impact. If your worry is gravel on the expressway, what you want is paint protection film, a physical layer. Anyone who tells you a coating makes paint chip-proof is selling you something. It also will not hide existing swirl marks: those get machine-polished out first, which is why correction is in the price rather than extra.

They solve different problems. A coating is a few microns thick: it changes how the surface behaves chemically, so you get gloss, water that sheets off and far less etching. PPF is a physical film measured in tenths of a millimetre: it absorbs stone chips. If your car lives on the expressway and you care about chips, you want film, usually on the front end only. If you want the paint to stay sharp and easy to wash, you want a coating. Plenty of people end up with film on the nose and coating over everything.
