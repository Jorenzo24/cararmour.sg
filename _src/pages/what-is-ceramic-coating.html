A layer thinner than a hair, bonded to the clear coat

A ceramic coating is a liquid resin built mostly on silicon dioxide, the compound glass and quartz are made of. It is applied by hand, one panel at a time. Once its solvent evaporates, the resin reacts with moisture in the air and cross-links into a transparent film that is chemically bonded to the clear coat, the top layer of your factory paint.

The film is very thin. One layer is around a micron, while the clear coat beneath it usually measures 35 to 55 microns and a human hair about 70. You cannot see or feel the added thickness. What changes is the surface: smoother and harder than bare clear coat, and far less inclined to hold on to water and dirt.

In practice, rain beads up and runs off instead of drying into spots. Bird droppings, tree sap and road film sit on the coating rather than keying into the paint, so most of them come off with a normal wash.

The clear coat is the layer every polish removes a little of, and it cannot grow back. A coating takes the daily wear in its place, and it can be topped up or reapplied when it thins.

What a ceramic coating will not do

  1. Stop stone chips

    At around a micron, a coating is far too thin to absorb an impact. Paint protection film, a polyurethane layer of roughly 150 to 200 microns, is the product made for that.

    Ceramic coating · about 1 µm
    Paint protection film · about 200 µm

  2. Make paint scratch‑proof

    The 9H printed on many bottles is a grade on the pencil hardness test (ASTM D3363). Leads of increasing hardness are pushed across a coated laboratory panel; 9H means the hardest one did not cut through the film. It says nothing about a key or a careless wash.

  3. Hide swirl marks

    A coating preserves whatever lies underneath it, swirls included. That is why the paint is machine-polished first, and why the polishing decides the final finish.

How a ceramic coating is applied

A careful application takes several hours, and most of that time goes into preparing the paint.

  1. Wash and decontamination

    A pH-neutral wash, then a chemical treatment that dissolves iron fallout, and a clay pass that lifts what is still stuck to the paint.

  2. Machine polishing

    Swirl marks and water spots are polished out of the clear coat. The coating will preserve whatever it covers, so this is the step that decides the final finish. More on polishing.

  3. Panel wipe

    Polishing oils are removed with a dedicated cleaner, so the coating bonds to bare clear coat and not to a film of residue.

  4. Application, panel by panel

    The coating is spread in small sections. Each one is left to flash for a moment, then levelled with a microfibre so no high spots remain.

  5. Final inspection

    Every panel is checked under strong light for high spots or missed areas before the car goes back on the road.

How long a ceramic coating lasts

Professional ceramic coatings are usually rated for two to five years. That rating assumes good preparation and sensible care. On a real car, where it lands in the range comes down to a handful of factors.

  • Preparation. The coating bonds only to clean, polished clear coat. Residue left underneath shortens its life from the first day.
  • Number of layers. A second layer adds durability, most usefully on the bonnet, roof and boot lid, which take the most sun.
  • Where the car is parked. A car left in an open-air carpark in Singapore takes far more UV and rain than one under cover.
  • How it is washed. Brush washes and strong detergents wear the layer down faster than anything else.
  • Top-ups. A silica spray applied every few months renews the surface and spares the base layer.

Looking after a coated car

The coating does most of the work. A few habits decide how long it keeps performing.

  1. First 24 hours

    Keep it dry

    The coating is still curing. Rain on a fresh coating can leave marks in the surface.

  2. First week

    Water only

    Rinse without shampoo. Most coatings take about a week to reach full hardness.

  3. Every wash

    Gentle washing

    pH-neutral shampoo, two buckets, a clean microfibre to dry. Automated brush washes are best avoided.

  4. Every few months

    Maintenance top-up

    A decontamination wash and a silica top-up spray renew the surface. Coat maintenance.
